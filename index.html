VIA vs Austin Robotaxi / Uber

Full cost-per-mile comparison · November 2026

What this shows

VIA’s entire budget (operating + amortization + capital) allocated over the vehicle miles and boardings required to carry passengers, compared with the prices riders actually pay for robotaxi and Uber in Austin.

Capital and overhead are included because they are part of the resources needed to put buses on the street and people on them.

VIA key figures (San Antonio)

  • FY2025 actual: 31.2 million passengers · 32.8 million vehicle miles · 2.4 million hours
  • NTD 2024: Operating expenses $262.6M · Vehicle revenue miles 32.1M · Passenger-miles 152M · Unlinked trips 30.7M
  • FY2026 budget: Operating $339M (includes ~$22M amortization) · Capital $235M · Combined ~$574M
  • Average trip length: ~5 passenger-miles per boarding
  • Average bus load: ~6.7 passengers on board (all-day average)

Takeaways

  • On pure operating cost per passenger-mile, VIA is lower (~$1.73) because one bus mile serves multiple riders.
  • Once the full budget required to keep the system running and growing is allocated, a typical 5-mile VIA trip costs roughly $10–18 — in the same range as current Austin robotaxi and Uber fares.
  • Robotaxi and Uber prices are what riders actually pay for door-to-door, on-demand service. VIA fares recover only a small fraction of total cost; the rest comes from sales tax and grants.
  • Average bus load of ~6–7 people means most capacity is unused on an all-day basis. Higher utilization would lower VIA’s cost per passenger further.
  • Current capital spending is high due to the rapid-transit lines. In a lower-capital year the fully loaded figure would be closer to the operating-plus-amortization number.

Caveats

  • Miles and boardings are system-wide averages; individual routes vary.
  • Allocating one year’s full capital budget is a high-end view of the resources required to sustain and expand the network (capital is multi-year and partly grant-funded).
  • Austin robotaxi prices are dynamic snapshots from trackers and rider reports, not official rate cards.
  • VIA and Austin are different cities — this is a cost-structure comparison, not a direct service match.
  • Robotaxis currently serve a smaller geography and different demand pattern than a full fixed-route network.
  • The robotaxi and Uber figures assume one passenger per ride. Shared or multi-passenger trips would lower the effective cost per person further.
